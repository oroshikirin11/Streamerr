Never a dead buttonThe browser hides screen sharing on plain http. Instead of a button that does nothing, the page explains and offers the fix.
Self-served httpsStreamerr generates a certificate and listens on 8443. No proxy needed; the public VPS route would hairpin the capture through the home upload.
Decided by isSecureContextOne check picks this state. Behind a proper proxy the page never shows it.
One actionShare screen. The browser's own picker chooses the surface; we open it on monitors first and hide the Streamerr tab from the list.
The delay is said up frontNo delay by default, and the page says what that costs. Slack is a Timing choice, never a surprise.
Settings in a plain rowAudio, quality, frame rate, and what happens when sharing ends. Defaults work; nothing has to be touched.
See it before viewers doThe picked stream plays locally at zero cost. Wrong monitor? Cancel and pick again.
Armed, not liveOne way in: Go live. It is refused, with the reason, while anything else is on air — a share is its own broadcast. The screen stays picked meanwhile.
The audio truthThe caption says “no audio” and why, with the fix, rather than letting the operator find out from a viewer.
Transport barRed-free: LIVE uses the panel's on-air green. Elapsed time instead of a duration; ±30 s disabled because there is nothing to seek in. Pause and Skip work as always.
Health in one lineSending rate, backlog, whether the box keeps up, and how far behind viewers are. Amber when the backlog grows, red when the box falls under 1×.
Pause is the shieldThe existing hold card, relabelled “Back in a moment”. Resume re-attaches to the live feed. No new control to learn.
Sidebar chip“Sharing screen” under the on-air badge, visible from every page — the operator will be in the Studio dragging overlays.
Phone or second panelThe picture lives in the sharing browser; every other panel sees status and can pause or stop. Stop closes the socket; the sharing tab tears down on its own.
